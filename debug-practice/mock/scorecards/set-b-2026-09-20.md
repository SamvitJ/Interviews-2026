## What to work on

**When the bug is recognized instantly, the reasoning stops — and the parts the
pattern-match didn't cover go unexamined.** On both m1 and m3 the fix arrived
before the evidence did. That works when the bug is already known; it leaves
nothing to fall back on when it isn't.

- m1: asked three times why `200 is ok` passes. Candidate did not know the
  answer (small-integer caching) — this was a knowledge gap, not evasion. The
  scoring issue is how the gap was signaled: the reply was "before I answer
  that, I printed...", a deferral to a tangent, rather than "I don't know."
  An explicit "I don't know" is the stronger move — it is fast, honest, and
  tells the interviewer where to spend the remaining time. Interviewers are
  calibrating behavior at the edge of knowledge; deflecting reads as hedging
  even when it isn't.
  Clock note: interviewer gave the 7-minute warning first and proposed moving
  on; candidate pushed back; interviewer agreed to stay; candidate then elected
  to move on. Time pressure originated with the interviewer.
- m3: said `replace_tags` "reassigns the global list." It creates an instance
  attribute that shadows the class one. Corrected cleanly — but only after
  being handed the experiment. Writing that check unprompted is the 2 -> 3.

Habit to build: **after finding a bug, explain every passing test.** If you
can't, the mechanism isn't fully in hand yet. In a real round the current
pattern reads as fast but shallow, which is what a senior interviewer probes
for deliberately.

Secondary: m1 burned 9 minutes on a 4-minute bug, which compressed m4. Also
said "we shouldn't move on until we're done," then moved on two messages later
without answering — fine as a decision, worth making deliberately.

Looking up lock syntax and the GIL was correct. API recall isn't the skill
under test and saying so plainly beats bluffing.

## Post-session

Worked out the m1 follow-up after the debrief: two *independent* implementation
details can make `is` on ints accidentally return True — small-integer caching
(-5..256) and per-code-object constant deduplication in `co_consts`. The second
is why a script and the REPL disagree on `c = 257; d = 257; c is d`. Neither is
in the language spec.
